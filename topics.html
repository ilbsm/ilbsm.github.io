Protein Folding & Energy Landscapes
How do knotted proteins fold reliably? How does topology affect mechanical stability?

Proteins with non-trivial topology — knots, slipknots, and lassos — represent one of the most fascinating puzzles in structural biology. How does a linear polymer chain tie itself into a knot during folding without getting permanently stuck?

We build multidimensional models of the folding energy landscape using coarse-grained Go-models and maximum entropy approaches. Our central finding: knotted proteins possess topological barriers that protect their function and create unique mechanical signatures detectable by single-molecule force spectroscopy.

02
Bioinformatics & Structure Prediction
Detecting protein topology at proteome scale — from sequence co-evolution to 3D knot classification
Phylogenetic conservation of protein knots
MSA alignment · conservation · contact map prediction

We develop the computational infrastructure that makes proteome-scale topological analysis possible. Our databases — KnotProt, AlphaKnot, LassoProt, LinkProt — catalogue topological complexity across hundreds of thousands of protein structures.

With the AlphaFold revolution, we were the first to analyze the predicted human proteome for knots, discovering new knot types including the rare 6₃ stevedore knot never seen in experimental structures. Direct Coupling Analysis (DCA) extracts evolutionary co-variation signals to predict 3D contacts from sequence alone.

03
Applied Mathematics & Knot Theory
Developing the mathematical language for biological topology — from knot invariants to Seifert surfaces

Classifying protein topology requires rigorous mathematics. We collaborate with mathematicians to develop and apply knot invariants — polynomial signatures (Alexander, Jones, HOMFLY) that uniquely identify knot types in biological macromolecules.

Unlike mathematical knots which form closed loops, protein chains have free ends. We develop open-chain topology formalisms, extending to Seifert surfaces and genus theory to describe the topological complexity of both proteins and nucleic acids. Our Topoly Python package makes these invariants widely accessible.

04
Biochemistry & Drug Design
Knotted enzymes as antibiotic targets — from TrmD to SARS-CoV-2 protease inhibition
Knotted fold vs. unknotted fold — TrmD
Antimicrobial drug targets

The most compelling biochemical consequence of protein knotting is its direct role in catalysis. The TrmD enzyme — a tRNA methyltransferase essential for bacterial translation — owes its activity to a trefoil knot at its active site, creating a geometry impossible in unknotted proteins.

This makes TrmD a compelling antibiotic target with no structural human homologue. We apply DCA-guided computational docking and all-atom simulations to discover inhibitors for knotted proteins implicated in cancer, neurodegeneration (Parkinson — UCH-L1), and viral infection (SARS-CoV-2 papain-like protease).

Funded Research Projects

Our research is supported by grants from EMBO, the Polish National Science Centre (NCN), Foundation for Polish Science (FNP), COST, and the European Union. Below are the major projects that have shaped the lab's research programme.

EMBO
AIM SQuaRE · Caltech · 2024
AIM SQuaRE Program
Collaboration at the American Institute of Mathematics on the topology of biological polymers. Brings together mathematicians and biophysicists to develop new invariants for protein topology classification.
EMBO
Installation Grant · PI: Sulkowska
Comprehensive Analysis of Knotted Proteins
Understanding the role of knots in proteins by means of computer simulations and experimental techniques. Focus on the TrmD knotted methyltransferase and its role in bacterial translation fidelity.
EMBO
Small Grant · PI: Sulkowska
Synthesis of Inhibitors for TrmD Proteins
Discovering selective inhibitors for the knotted TrmD tRNA methyltransferase. First step: synthesizing and testing ligands suggested as selective by AstraZeneca. Collaboration with the Jacek Jemielity group.
NCN
Sonata BIS · PI: Sulkowska
Influence of Knotted Structure on Protein Function
Understanding the role and function of knots in proteins. The central hypothesis: knot geometry correlates with active site location, molecular motion, and co-evolving amino acid pairs. Combines theory with DCA-based structure prediction.
NCN
Preludium · PI: Dąbrowski-Tumański
Identification of Proteins with Link Topology
Automatic detection of link topology in protein structures, topological classification, and creation of the LinkProt database. Uses mathematical tools from knot theory and theory of minimal surfaces to study folding of linked proteins.
FNP
INTER grant · PI: Niemyska
Can Biology Be Caught on a Mathematical Lasso?
Using theory from mathematics and physics — characteristics of curves and surfaces in 3D space — to create tools for uncovering the folding of recently discovered proteins with lasso topology.
COST
EUTOPIA Network · PI: Sulkowska
Classification and Free Energy Landscape of Entanglement Proteins
Going beyond knotted proteins to include complex lasso proteins and links. Understanding the energy landscape of tangled structures — knots, slipknots, lassos — using mechanical manipulation models verified by experiment.
ICM
Computational Grant · PI: Sulkowska
Great Challenges — TrmD Ligand Binding
Investigating the influence of non-trivial topology on ligand binding in the TrmD family. The knotted active site directly affects protein function through interactions with ligands. Collaboration with the Ya-Ming Hou group, Thomas Jefferson University, USA.
UW
DSM Grant · PI: Dąbrowski-Tumański
Structure-Function Relation in Proteins with Lassos
Characterization of the structure-function relationship in proteins with lasso topology. Development of methods to study lasso motifs and their functional implications — leading to the LassoProt database.
